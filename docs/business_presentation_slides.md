# ACT II: THE FALSE HOPE OF NEW BUILDS

## Slide 4: Empirical Construction Realities: 50 Years of Lead-Time Data
* **Key Visual:** **Figure 1: Empirical Construction Duration Distributions** (`outputs/figures/fig1_construction_durations.png`).
* **The Data Reality vs. Vendor Pitchbooks:**
  * **Vendor Pitchbooks:** Routinely promise 5 to 7-year turnkey construction schedules.
  * **Empirical Reality (424 Commercial Operating Reactors):**
    * **Historical Mean Lead Time:** **8.76 years** (Standard Deviation: 5.32 years).
    * **Empirical Median Lead Time:** **7.16 years** (IQR: 5.66 to 10.18 years).
    * **90th Percentile:** **13.52 years**.
  * **The Western Disconnect:** While serial construction in Asia achieves 6-year builds, North America (median 10.2 years) and Western Europe (median 9.4 years) exhibit extreme tail delays (Flamanville-3: 17y, Olkiluoto-3: 18y, Vogtle-3&4: 14y).
  * **Advisory Conclusion:** Multi-year schedule slippage is not an unforeseen 'black swan'; it is the **statistical baseline** of Western megaproject delivery.
* **Speaker Pitch:**
  > *"When an engineering contractor tells you they will build an EPR or AP1000 in 60 months, show them this empirical distribution. Across 424 commercial reactors, the historical mean is nearly 9 years. In Western Europe and the US, a 12- to 14-year build is typical. Any decarbonization strategy that assumes new reactors will arrive on time to replace the 181 GW cliff by 2035 is scientifically and statistically invalid."*

# ACT III: THE CAPITAL COMPOUNDING ENGINE

## Slide 6: The Financing Penalty: How Delays Compound Debt (IDC)
* **Key Visual:** **Figure 3: Capex Escalation & IDC Compounding Curves** (`outputs/figures/fig3_idc_compounding_escalation.png`).
* **The Financial Compounding Mechanism:**
  * **S-Curve Cash Flows:** Annual disbursements follow a $\text{Beta}(2.5, 2.5)$ civil engineering curve.
  * **Discrete Mid-Year Compounding:**
    $$\text{Capex}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{t=1}^T \left[ w_t \cdot \text{Capex}_{\text{esc}} \cdot (1 + r)^{T - t + 0.5} \right]$$
  * **The Capital Erosion Shock:**
    * *On-Time @ 5.5% WACC (Regulated Utility):* \$7,500/kW $\to$ **\$8,890/kW** (+18.5% IDC).
    * *On-Time @ 10.0% WACC (Merchant Financing):* \$7,500/kW $\to$ **\$10,297/kW** (+37.3% IDC).
    * *+5y Delay @ 7.0% WACC (Baseline Market):* \$7,500/kW $\to$ **\$12,713/kW** (+69.5% IDC).
    * *+7y Delay @ 10.0% WACC (High Friction):* \$7,500/kW $\to$ **\$17,358/kW** (+131.4% IDC).
  * **Core Insight:** Financing charges during delays exceed the physical cost of the reactor itself, destroying equity balance sheets.
* **Speaker Pitch:**
  > *"Here is the financial engine that broke Westinghouse and Areva. During construction, the plant generates zero revenue while drawing billions in debt. When schedule slips from 7 to 14 years in a 10% rate environment, total capitalized cost explodes to \$17,358 per kilowatt. Interest does not sleep. The debt compounds exponentially and eats the balance sheet alive."*

## Slide 9: Analytical Boundaries & Study Limitations
* **Key Visual:** A structured governance table detailing methodological boundaries and risk mitigations.
* **Transparent Limitations of Our Study:**
  1. **Data Completeness & Reporting Lag:** The GEM Tracker relies on reported utility filings; older units built in the 1970s often report commercial operation by year or year-month, standardized to mid-year/mid-month ($\pm 3$ month margin).
  2. **Generic Overnight Capex Benchmarks:** Modeled figures (\$1,200/kW LTO, \$7,500/kW Gen-III+) represent industry averages and do not capture unit-specific metallurgy or unique containment geometry.
  3. **100% CCGT Replacement Simplification:** We model full replacement by natural gas CCGT as an empirical proxy for firm baseload. While renewables + battery storage will capture part of this generation, lack of multi-day seasonal storage forces grids to burn natural gas to maintain grid inertia.
  4. **Regulatory Approval Hurdles:** 20-Year LTO requires decennial safety reviews (*visites décennales* by ASN in France, Subsequent License Renewals by the US NRC) and public hearings. Economic superiority does not bypass safety constraints.
  5. **Nuclear Fuel Cycle & High-Level Waste:** Extending reactor lifetimes increases the inventory of spent nuclear fuel requiring long-term deep geological repositories (e.g. Cigéo in France, Onkalo in Finland).
* **Speaker Pitch:**
  > *"As responsible advisors, we must be completely candid about the boundaries of our data. Life extension is not an automatic rubber stamp: it requires rigorous ten-year safety reviews by independent regulators, continued investment in deep geological waste disposal like Cigéo, and careful component inspections. However, within these bounds, the strategic case for LTO remains unassailable."*
