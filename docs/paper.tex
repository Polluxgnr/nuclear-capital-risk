\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Abstract}---Nuclear energy provides reliable low-carbon baseload electricity, but new builds suffer from multi-year delays and severe Interest During Construction (IDC) compounding. Analyzing the August 2026 Global Nuclear Power Tracker (1,825 reactors), we find that \textbf{180.99\,GW across 198 operating reactors (44.38\% of global operating capacity)} has reached or exceeded its 40-year design lifetime. Historical construction lead times average \textbf{8.76 years} (median: 7.16 years), making delays the empirical baseline. Retiring this fleet would trigger an avoided emissions loss of \textbf{558.1\,Mt\,CO$_2$/year} if replaced by natural gas CCGT. We model project finance cash flows across Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) scenarios ($r \in [4\%, 10\%]$). We find that 20-year Long-Term Operation (LTO / life extension) at \$1,200/kW yields an ultra-stable Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) of \textbf{\$40.47 to \$48.98/MWh}. In contrast, new-build Gen-III+ reactors (\$7,500/kW overnight) suffer dramatic debt compounding: at 7\% WACC with a 5-year delay, capex balloons to \$12,713/kW and LCOE to \$138.14/MWh. Under 10\% merchant WACC with a 7-year delay, Gen-III+ LCOE reaches \$204.60/MWh (4.6$\times$ LTO). We conclude that fleet life extension provides a \textbf{\$1.14 Trillion capital arbitrage} over new builds.
\end{abstract}

% --- Section 1: Introduction ---
\section{Introduction \& Transition Context}
Decarbonizing power grids requires firm, low-carbon generation to complement non-synchronous wind and solar resources \cite{ipcc2022mitigation}. Nuclear fission generates zero operational emissions ($<12\,\text{gCO}_2\text{eq/kWh}$), yet private capital deployment into greenfield reactors is constrained by severe financial friction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Capital Lumpiness}: Greenfield GW-scale stations require upfront capital exceeding \$15B--\$25B.
    \item \textbf{Systemic Construction Delays}: Historical megaprojects routinely double initial budget schedules.
    \item \textbf{Compounding Cost of Capital}: Interest During Construction (IDC) accumulates exponentially during unproductive building years, magnified by post-2022 rate hikes \cite{damodaran2026wacc}.
    \item \textbf{The Retirement Cliff}: Fleets constructed in the 1970s and 1980s face their 40-year licensing thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Core Question:} \textit{Does long-term operation (LTO) of aging reactors offer a financially superior risk-adjusted return over new-build investments under rising cost-of-capital scenarios?}

\subsection{Empirical Construction Lead Times}
We compute historical lead times as $\Delta t = (\text{COD} - \text{Start})/365.25$. As shown in \cref{fig:lead_times}, completed reactors exhibit an empirical mean lead time of \textbf{8.76 years} and a median of \textbf{7.16 years} (IQR: 5.66 to 10.18 years). Eastern Asia achieves disciplined builds (median: 5.8 years), whereas North America (median: 10.2 years) and Western Europe (median: 9.4 years) exhibit extreme right-skewed tail risks.

    \item \textbf{SMR FOAK}: Overnight capex \$9,000/kW, nominal time $T_0 = 4.0$ years, lifetime $N = 40$ years.
    \item \textbf{20-Yr LTO (Grand Car{\'e}nage)}: Overnight capex \$1,200/kW, outage time $T_0 = 2.0$ years, extended lifetime $N = 20$ years.
\end{itemize}

% --- EQUATION 1: S-Curve Cumulative Spend Distribution ---
\subsection{S-Curve Capital Spend Profile}
Capital disbursement follows a continuous Beta cumulative distribution function over normalized time $\tau = t / T \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
S(\tau) = \frac{1}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha, \beta)} \int_0^\tau u^{\alpha-1} (1-u)^{\beta-1} \, \mathrm{d}u
\label{eq:scurve}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha = 2.5$ and $\beta = 2.5$. The discrete annual spending weight is $w_t = S(t/T) - S((t-1)/T)$.

% --- EQUATION 2: Discrete IDC Compounding Equation ---
\subsection{Interest During Construction (IDC)}
For a construction duration $T = T_0 + \Delta t$ under a Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) $r$, annual disbursements compound forward to commercial operation:
\begin{equation}
\text{Capex}_{\text{total}}(T, r) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} w_t \cdot \text{Capex}_{\text{esc}} \cdot (1 + r)^{T - t + 0.5}
\label{eq:idc}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Capex}_{\text{esc}} = \text{Capex}_{\text{overnight}} \cdot (1 + 0.015 \cdot \Delta t)$ captures a 1.5\% annual site carrying cost escalation during delays.

% --- EQUATION 3: LCOE and Capital Recovery Factor (CRF) ---
\subsection{Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE)}
The Levelized Cost of Electricity balances discounted lifecycle capital and operating costs against total generation:
\begin{equation}
\text{LCOE} = \frac{\text{CRF}(r, N) \cdot \text{Capex}_{\text{total}} + \text{Fixed O\&M}}{8760 \cdot \text{CF}} + \text{VarCost}
\label{eq:lcoe}
\end{equation}
where $\text{CRF}(r, N) = \frac{r(1+r)^N}{(1+r)^N - 1}$ is the Capital Recovery Factor, $\text{CF} = 0.88$ is the capacity factor, and $\text{VarCost} = \text{Var O\&M} + \text{Fuel Cost}$.

\subsection{LCOE Superiority of Fleet Life Extension}
As presented in \cref{fig:lcoe_chart} and \cref{tab:lcoe_table}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{LTO Resilience}: LTO generation cost remains between **\$40.47 and \$48.98/MWh** across all WACCs, well below wholesale power benchmarks (\$60--\$90/MWh).
    \item \textbf{New-Build Penalty}: On-time Gen-III+ produces electricity at \$113.01/MWh at 7\% WACC (2.54$\times$ LTO). With a 5-year delay, generation costs rise to \$138.14/MWh (3.11$\times$ LTO).
    \item \textbf{SMR FOAK Economics}: SMRs produce power at \$128.65/MWh at 7\% WACC, failing to beat LTO economics prior to factory learning curves.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Decarbonization Levers \& Policy Playbook}
\textit{(Sustainability Consultant Deliverable)}

\subsection{The Carbon Opportunity Cost}
Retiring the 180.99\,GW cliff fleet forces replacement with natural gas CCGT ($400\,\text{gCO}_2/\text{kWh}$ at an 88\% CF), injecting:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \text{CO}_2 = 180.99 \times 10^6 \times 8760 \times 0.88 \times 400 \times 10^{-12} = \mathbf{558.1\,\text{Mt/yr}}
\end{equation}
into the atmosphere. This avoided carbon cliff exceeds the entire national annual emissions of France or the UK, and is equivalent to adding \textbf{121.3 Million passenger vehicles} to the road. 

\textit{Methodological Justification:} In high-voltage grids, intermittent renewables cannot replace synchronous thermal inertia without unbuilt multi-day battery or hydrogen storage. Empirically (e.g. California post-San Onofre, Germany post-Atomausstieg), retiring nuclear reactors is offset by gas CCGT running at baseload availability.

\subsection{Strategic Decarbonization Levers}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prioritize LTO First}: Refurbishing the 181\,GW cliff fleet costs \$217 billion, generating a **\$1.14 Trillion capital saving** over greenfield replacement capacity.
    \item \textbf{Regulated Asset Base (RAB)}: Allowing utilities to charge consumers financing costs during construction compresses WACC from 8.5\% to 4.5\%, removing IDC debt compounding.
    \item \textbf{Contracts for Difference (CfD)}: Index-linked revenue floors insulate utilities from merchant volatility.
    \item \textbf{Green Taxonomy Alignment}: Qualification under EU Sustainable Finance unlocks low-cost ESG debt, compressing spreads by 40--60\,bps.
\end{enumerate}

% --- Section 6: Conclusion ---
\section{Conclusion}
Fleet life extension (LTO) offers the lowest-cost, lowest-risk climate finance option for low-carbon baseload electricity. Under rising interest rates and empirical construction delays, new builds suffer acute capital erosion. Preserving the 181\,GW aging fleet must be the immediate focus for utility capital allocation and energy policy.
